Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Price per towel matters more than the set price. A $46 pack of two costs $23 each, while a $39.99 six-piece set drops closer to $7 per piece. Do the division before you compare the best budget hand towels.
  • 100% cotton wins for absorbency. Six of our seven picks use 100% cotton. It pulls water off your hands faster than most blends and stays that way wash after wash.
  • Skip fabric softener. It coats the cotton loops and slowly kills absorbency. Half a dose of detergent and a medium-heat dry keep budget towels soft for years.
  • Sets vs singles. Multi-piece sets like Madison Park and American Soft Linen give you matched hand towels and washcloths at the lowest per-piece cost. Singles like Hudson Baby suit you if you only need one or two.
  • Weight tells you the feel. Heavier GSM cotton, like the BIOWEAVES 700, feels plush and takes longer to dry on the rack. Lighter towels dry faster but feel thinner.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I spend on a good hand towel?

You can buy a solid hand towel for $5 to $10 per piece. In our test, the best budget hand towels ranged from the $12.46 Hudson Baby single up to the $46 BIOWEAVES pack of two, which works out to $23 each. Above roughly $15 per towel you mostly pay for thicker pile and organic certification, not better drying.

Is 100% cotton better than a cotton blend for hand towels?

For drying your hands, 100% cotton absorbs faster and holds more water than most blends. Six of our seven picks use 100% cotton for that reason. The one cotton-blend pick, the Buttons and Stitches hooded set, trades a little absorbency for a soft, lightweight feel that suits baby and toddler skin.

How do I keep budget hand towels soft after washing?

Wash on warm, skip fabric softener, and use about half the detergent you would for clothes. Softener coats cotton loops and cuts absorbency over time. Dry on medium heat and pull the towels out while they still feel slightly damp. Every budget hand towel in our test stayed soft through repeated washes when we followed that routine.

Are bigger sets better value than buying single towels?

Usually, yes. The eight-piece White Classic set drops to about $5 per towel, and the six-piece Madison Park and American Soft Linen sets land near $7 each. A single like the Hudson Baby makes sense only if you need one or two towels rather than a full bathroom's worth.
